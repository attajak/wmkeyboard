**The first public release.** Everything runs on the device: no account, no
server, nothing leaves the phone unless you ask a network tool for something.
What follows is what changed since 0.2.0. The keyboard itself was built over
[0.1.0](https://github.com/wasi-master/wmkeyboard/releases/tag/v0.1.0) and
[0.2.0](https://github.com/wasi-master/wmkeyboard/releases/tag/v0.2.0).

### Glide typing, rewritten

- **The swipe decoder walks the dictionary instead of scanning it.**
- **Every language with a word list can be swiped**, not only English, and
  Avro swipes in Latin while committing Bengali.
- The stroke's *shape* is scored, not just where it was drawn.
- **A swipe that cannot be read asks instead of guessing.**
- A glided word brings its own space.

### Bengali

A downloadable n-gram pack, which answers to either spelling of a nukta.

### Backup

**The keyboard backs itself up, to seven places**, with network and charging
gates, a freer schedule, safer wipes, and typing statistics travelling in the
bundle.

### Tablets and big screens

**The keyboard fills a big screen instead of stretching to it**, with the
numbers moved to where they belong on a wide board.

### Hardware keyboards

The focus ring learns Home, End and paging, and reaches the AI, translate,
grammar, voice, calendar, camera, media, generator and plugin panels. Physical
keys type into the calculator and converters, reach the AI custom-instruction
box, and a chord changes the input language.

### Themes and appearance

- **One theme, many looks.** Variant families, with gradients getting their own
  family and dots showing each look.
- A theme can name a font per script, and resolve a font or sound by its
  installed name.
- The panels stop dressing themselves; menus, cards, popups and previews all
  follow the theme honestly.
- **A font library for every script.**

### Sound

Key sounds can have more than one recording, and sound packs play the key
coming back up, previewable before install.

### Voice

Dictation collapses to a floating bar you can drag, with inline surface
switching and a slide transition. (Two intermediate reworks were reverted
along the way; the settings choice came back.)

### Emoji

The app fetches a current emoji font rather than asking you for one, and
notices when a newer one exists. Panel grid size and emoji size are adjustable.

### AI

A full chat screen under Tools → AI, with chats you can keep or delete and
Wi-Fi-only downloads.

### Tools and chips

- **Contextual chips**: dates, weather, lookups and tool hints.
- **A one-time code goes in one box at a time.**
- Unit chips walk the full degradation ladder; a metre answers in feet and
  inches; coins convert like currencies.
- An inline drag-resize tool, Gboard style.
- The typing test hands out badges, and the keyboard counts your typing.

### Languages

**The field's language is detected, and the keyboard leans into it.**

### Imports

**Read FlorisBoard and HeliBoard files**, and a keymap's name, language and
typing method move out of the JSON.

### Onboarding

A setup you can get through: the wizard learns who it is talking to and shows
its own length honestly.

### Privacy

**Every permission the keyboard holds, on one screen**, with every AI provider
named in the disclosures and the missing hosts and conditions filled in.

### Settings

An audit, and the ~50 settings it found missing: twelve hard-coded typing and
key-press values, eleven more across layout and appearance, a learn-a-word
threshold, a way to empty the blacklist, personal-dictionary search, emoji
recents length and history clearing, image results per row, metered downloads,
romanized auto-pairing, Morse pause, snippet order, fuzzy pinyin pair by pair,
a symbol row you can size, six tool limits, and **per-row reset to default**
everywhere. Screens stop composing before their first frame.

### Easter eggs

The settings app keeps a birthday and hides a game, the password panel cites
its sources, the QR scanner recognises *that* link, and the morse strip
acknowledges an SOS. All of it is spoiled on one documentation page.

### Size and packaging

Channel-aware ML Kit, a slimmer Harper, opt-in ABI-split APKs, and the LiteRT-LM
runtime delivered on demand for Play installs.

### Release pipeline

The release pipeline for GitHub, F-Droid and Play, and the version living in
one place.
