Translate opens a small typing window right above the keys. Type a sentence, watch the translation appear as you go, then send it to your field with a tap. It never reads what's already in the field. It translates whatever you type into the panel itself, so it works the same in a chat app, a note, or a search bar.

## Using it

Open **Translate** from the toolbar, or from the toolbox if you haven't pinned it (it isn't pinned by default). On a hardware keyboard, use the [leader key](/reference/shortcuts/): double-tap <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>, then press <KeyCap>R</KeyCap>.

The panel has its own search bar at the top. What you type there is what gets translated, not the text already in your field. It's the same key-rerouting trick the GIF, sticker, and Wikipedia panels use: while the panel is focused, your keystrokes go to its search bar instead of the app behind the keyboard. Opening Translate over a chat you're mid-way through never touches what you already wrote. The panel always starts empty and pre-fills from nothing.

The header reads left to right like a sentence: *from* this language, *to* that one, *using* this engine.

- **Source language** is the first chip. It starts on **Detect language** and switches to the language it found once a result comes back, for example "Bengali". Tap it to pick the source yourself when the detection is wrong, which mostly happens with a word or two. Your pick lasts until you close the panel. Pick **Detect language** to hand it back.
- **Swap** is the arrows between the chips. It turns the translation round: the result becomes the text, its language the source, and the language it was read as the new target. It lights up once there is a result to swap.
- **Target language** is the second chip. Tap it to open a dropdown of all 63 languages and pick one.
- **Engine** is the chip on the right, <Flavor edition="full" /> only: **Online**, **On device** or **Automatic**. See [On-device translation](#on-device-translation) below.
- The translation updates automatically about 400 milliseconds after you stop typing. Pressing return translates immediately, and changing the target language re-translates what's already in the box right away.
- Once a translation is showing, two actions light up below it:
  - **Insert** types the translation into your field at the cursor, leaving whatever was already there in place.
  - **Replace text** swaps out the field's *entire* contents for the translation. Use this when the field only holds the sentence you were translating and you want a clean swap rather than an insertion.

Long input is capped: anything past 2,500 characters is silently cut off before it's sent, with no on-screen warning that it happened.

:::note
Hardware-keyboard focus-ring navigation reaches the whole panel: <KeyCap>Tab</KeyCap> and arrow-key cycling take in the header chips and the swap button, the open menu's rows, and the action buttons (or the download button while that is the offer), the same way they do in the GIF or search panels. One rough edge is left. <KeyCap>Esc</KeyCap> while a menu is open closes the whole panel rather than just the menu, so press <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> on the chip to bring the list back.
:::

## On-device translation

<Flavor edition="full" /> The Full edition can translate on the phone with Google's ML Kit, with no connection and without the text leaving the device. Pick the engine from the chip on the panel or under Options:

- **Online** (the default) is what Translate has always done: Google's endpoint, your Cloud key, or your LibreTranslate server. It knows the most languages and gives the best translations.
- **On device** never sends the text anywhere. It needs a model for each language, about 30 MB each, downloaded once.
- **Automatic** uses the device whenever the languages are downloaded, and the online service for everything else: a language with no model, a pair you have not downloaded, text the device cannot read. The engine chip's icon shows which one produced the result on screen.

**On Google Play, the engine is a download too.** The Play edition keeps ML Kit's translator out of the install, because it is about 16 MB that most people never use. **On device** is the Play edition's default engine, so the first translation offers the download, and picking **On device** or **Automatic** later asks Google Play for it straight away. The panel and the Tools / Translate page show that download and carry on by themselves when it lands, with no restart. If Play cannot deliver it, the panel says so and the button tries again. APKs from GitHub carry the engine inside and skip this step.

**Models.** Each language is one download, and every model translates to and from English. Bengali to German therefore needs both the Bengali and the German model and goes through English on the way, which costs some accuracy. English itself is part of the engine and is never downloaded. When a translation needs a model you do not have, the result area says which and how big, and the action row becomes a **Download** button. The same button cancels while the download runs. When the models arrive, the text you typed is translated at once. Downloads also show in the notification shade, and they carry on if you close the panel. With [data saver](/reference/settings/data-saver/) holding downloads on a metered connection, the button asks first and a second press is the yes.

**Which languages.** 59 of the 63: everything in the list except Malayalam, Nepali, Punjabi and Traditional Chinese (the Chinese model is Simplified). With the engine on **On device** or **Automatic**, the language menus mark downloaded languages with a tick and the ones the device cannot do with **Online only**, and list the downloaded ones first.

**A short list on the device.** With the engine on **On device**, each language menu lists only what it can use now: the downloaded languages (and any on their way), the languages you type in, and the current selection. **All languages**, the last row, opens the rest of that menu, so you can still pick a language and download it from the offer. The next menu opens short again. **Automatic** always lists every language, because it sends the ones you have not downloaded online. Both behaviours are switches under [Options](#options).

**Detecting the source.** ML Kit's translator has to be told what it is reading, so a separate on-device language identifier (bundled, no download) does the detecting. It is reliable on a sentence and shaky on a word or two. When it is not sure, the keyboard falls back on the languages you type in, the active one first, and shows the source with a question mark, as in "French?". Tap the chip to correct it. Hindi, Japanese, Russian, Arabic, Greek, Bulgarian or Chinese typed in Latin letters is recognised for what it is and refused on the device, because no model reads it; Automatic sends it online instead.

**What to expect.** On-device translation is good for messages and short text and noticeably less exact than the online service on long, technical or idiomatic text. Line breaks and blank lines survive: each line is translated on its own. The translators are unloaded when you close the panel, since a loaded language pair holds tens of megabytes of memory.

## DeepL

DeepL is an optional online service that you bring yourself, with your own DeepL API key. Nothing about it shows or runs until you add a key (or a server address) under [Options](#options). The keyboard has no DeepL key of its own.

- **Translate with DeepL.** With a key set, the online engine asks DeepL instead of Google or LibreTranslate. A small **DeepL** label beside the engine chip says when DeepL made the translation on screen. For a language pair that DeepL does not have, the keyboard uses the usual service for that pair and the label goes away. On **Automatic**, DeepL only takes the part that would otherwise go online.
- **DeepL Write.** Off until you turn it on. Write corrects and rewrites text in its own language: grammar, wording, style and tone. It adds a button to the [Grammar check](/smart/grammar/#deepl-write) panel and a **DeepL Write** action to [selection actions](/smart/selection-actions/). DeepL sells Write with API Pro plans only, so a Free key gets an error from it (Free keys still translate).
- **Which server.** DeepL API Free keys end in `:fx` and go to `api-free.deepl.com`; every other key goes to `api.deepl.com`. The keyboard picks the server from the key. **DeepL server** replaces both, for a proxy that you run and that answers DeepL's `/v2/translate` requests.
- **Limits.** The Free plan includes 500,000 characters each month. When the month's characters are used up, the panel says so.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Translate" />

- **Turn on**: already on, like every tool. Switching it off removes Translate from both the toolbar and the toolbox.
- **Icon colour**: only shown if [colorful tool icons](/tools/overview/) are turned on globally. It overrides Translate's icon color.
- **Keyword shortcut**: a **Trigger words** field pre-filled with "translate", plus a **Match capitals** switch that's off by default. Type the word on its own and the suggestion strip offers to open the tool. See [smart suggestion chips](/smart/chips/).
- **Translate into**: the target language, from the same 63-language list as the panel's picker. Defaults to **English**. Changing it here also changes what the panel opens to next time.
- **Translation engine**: <Flavor edition="full" /> **Online**, **On device** or **Automatic**. The same choice as the panel's engine chip. The default is **On device** on Google Play and **Online** in the APKs from GitHub.
- **Downloaded languages first**: <Flavor edition="full" /> on by default. On **On device** and **Automatic**, the panel's language menus start with the languages on the device. Off keeps the usual order: English and Bengali, then the rest by name.
- **Only downloaded languages**: <Flavor edition="full" /> on by default. On **On device**, the panel's language menus show the downloaded languages, the languages you type in and the current selection, with **All languages** at the end for the rest. Off lists every language.
- **Languages on the device**: <Flavor edition="full" /> the on-device models. The list starts with English (built in), the language you translate into, the languages you type in, and anything already downloaded or on its way. **More languages** below it folds out the rest. Each row downloads, cancels or deletes one model and shows its progress. A download started here shows as running in the panel, and the other way round.
- **LibreTranslate server**: F-Droid build only, blank by default. Paste the address of a LibreTranslate server, your own or one you trust. Translation goes there instead of Google, even if a Cloud Translation key is also set. An optional key field sits below it, for instances that ask for one. See [Servers](/reference/settings/servers/) for the same field shared across tools.
- **DeepL API key**: blank by default, and the switch that turns DeepL on. See [DeepL](#deepl). The key rows below it show only once a key or a server is set.
- **DeepL server (optional)**: blank by default, which means DeepL's own server for your kind of key. Paste an address to send DeepL requests to a proxy instead.
- **Translate with DeepL**: on by default. Off keeps the key for DeepL Write only, and translation goes to the usual service.
- **DeepL Write**: off by default. On adds DeepL Write to the Grammar check panel and to selection actions. Needs a DeepL API Pro key.
- **Writing style**: shown while DeepL Write is on. **As it is** (default), **Simple**, **Business**, **Academic**, **Casual**, **Friendly**, **Confident**, **Diplomatic** or **Enthusiastic**. A language that DeepL has no styles for gets a plain rewrite.
- **Cloud Translation API key (optional)**: blank by default. Paste a Google Cloud Translation API key here to use the official API instead of the free endpoint (see below). The field saves as you type, with no separate save button. Under it you'll read "The app uses your key" once you've typed one, "This field is blank. The app uses the key that is built into the app." if this build ships one, or "Without a key, translation uses the free public endpoint of Google" otherwise.

This screen also shows the caption WM Keyboard displays right on the settings page:

> The keyboard sends the text that you translate to Google either way, and only while the translate panel is open. The free endpoint is unofficial and rate limited. A Cloud Translation key makes it official and reliable. If you set up DeepL below, DeepL gets the text instead, and Google gets only the languages that DeepL does not have.

If you back up your settings with **Include API keys** turned on, a Translate key or DeepL key you've pasted in travels with the backup along with your GIF, search, and AI keys. Without that switch, neither leaves the phone in a backup.

## Details & edge cases

- **On the device, nothing is sent.** With the engine on **On device**, the text you type is translated by ML Kit on the phone and goes nowhere. The only network use is the model downloads, which come from Google's servers (`dl.google.com`) through Android's download manager, plus ML Kit's own [diagnostics](/privacy/network/). On **Automatic**, text goes online only when the device cannot translate it.
- **Google, or LibreTranslate on the F-Droid build.** On the online engine, with no key, WM Keyboard calls Google's free public translation endpoint. It's the same unofficial one browser translate extensions use: fine for personal typing volumes, with no uptime guarantee and its own rate limits. With a key, it calls the official Cloud Translation v2 API instead, billed to your Google Cloud project and reliable. The F-Droid build adds a third path: paste a LibreTranslate server's address under Options. Every translation then goes there instead of either Google option, even with a Cloud Translation key also set. Whichever is active, only the text you type into the panel is sent. Nothing else in the field is.
- **DeepL, when you set it up.** With a DeepL key, translations go to DeepL first (see [DeepL](#deepl)). A pair that DeepL turns down goes to Google or LibreTranslate as above, and DeepL is not asked about that pair again until the keyboard restarts. Any other DeepL error (a wrong key, a used-up month) shows in the panel and is not sent anywhere else.
- **Sent, precisely:** the text currently in the panel's search box (truncated to 2,500 characters), the target language code, the source language code if you picked one, and your key where one applies. DeepL receives the text in a POST body and the key in a request header. The free Google endpoint receives the text as part of the request URL. The official Google API and a configured LibreTranslate server both send it in a POST body, LibreTranslate's carrying its optional key alongside.
- **When it's sent:** automatically ~400ms after you stop typing, immediately on return or retry, and immediately again if you change the target language, all only while the panel is open. Closing the panel cancels any translation in flight.
- **Power Saving mode's "Background network" toggle doesn't touch this.** That toggle only stops fetches that happen without you asking: link previews, QR auto-scan, dictionary look-ups. Tools you open yourself, translate included, keep working even with it on.
- **Not available before your phone is unlocked once after a restart.** Translate isn't on the short list of tools that work on a locked device, and the on-device models live in storage that is locked until then. It won't appear if you're typing on the lock screen before that first unlock.
- **Online translation works the same in both editions.** The on-device engine is Full only, because ML Kit is. Lite and the F-Droid build have no engine chip and no model list, and translate online exactly as before.
- **Deleting a model** frees its space at once. If the panel was using it, the next translation asks for it again.
- If a request fails outright, from no connection, a bad key, or a timeout, the panel shows the raw error message rather than a simplified one. An offline failure can read as a technical exception string instead of "No internet connection."

Looking for something different named "translate"? The [AI tool](/tools/ai/) has its own Translate action, which uses an LLM for more natural phrasing than a literal translation. [Offline voice dictation](/tools/whisper/) has a separate "Translate to English" toggle for what you *say* rather than what you type. Both are configured on their own settings screens, independent of this one. For the full picture of what leaves your device and when, see [network policy](/privacy/network/).
