The suggestion blacklist is a list of words you never want offered back to you. A nickname you don't want autocompleted for a stranger, a correction you're tired of seeing, anything at all. Add a word to it and the keyboard keeps it out of the suggestion strip and out of autocorrect for good, while still letting you type and send it yourself whenever you choose to.

## Adding and removing words

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Suggestions / Suggestion blacklist" />

Tap **Add word** at the top of the editor. A dialog opens with a single **Word** field. Type the word, then confirm with **Add**. The **Add** button stays disabled until the field has something in it. The word is stored lowercased and trimmed, so capitalization and stray spaces in what you type don't matter.

The list itself is sorted alphabetically, with a plain trash icon on each row to remove one. Once the list has at least one word in it, a **Clear the blacklist** row appears above it, which empties the whole list after a confirmation. Past a dozen words a search field appears above the list, the same threshold the [personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) screen uses, and the list itself draws 100 rows at a time with a **Show N more words** row to reveal the rest, so a list hundreds of words long never composes all at once.

You can also add a word without leaving the keyboard. Press and hold it on the suggestion strip and pick **Never suggest** from the menu that opens. The same menu offers **Suggest again** for a word that is already on the list, so a slip is one hold away from being undone. The rest of that menu, and the word card behind its **Edit** item, are described on [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/#from-the-suggestion-strip).

<PhoneFrame caption="Adding a word to the blacklist from the Add word dialog.">
  ![Adding a word to the blacklist from the Add word dialog.](@assets/screens/smart/blacklist-add-dialog.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

Before you add anything, the screen shows "No words in this list yet. Add a word above." The row back on the Typing screen reads "Words to never suggest or autocorrect to." Once the list has entries, that row's subtitle switches to a running count instead, like "3 words never suggested."

## What gets filtered

A blacklisted word is matched case-insensitively and kept out of every place the keyboard offers a word back to you, well beyond the ordinary English suggestion strip:

- **Completions**: dictionary, custom dictionary, secondary-language, personal-dictionary, contact-name, contact-email, and app-name completions, plus missing-space split suggestions, are all filtered before they reach the strip.
- **Bangla (Avro) suggestions**: loanword-map hits, phonetic siblings, and the phonetic transliteration itself are filtered the same way, so the blacklist reaches transliterated composing too, not only plain-English typing. The composing preview that shows a listed spelling mid-word skips blacklisted spellings too.
- **Next-word predictions**: the empty-composing bigram predictions that appear before you've typed anything are filtered as well.
- **Glide typing**: a decoded stroke's candidates go through the same filter, so a blacklisted word can't arrive by swipe either.
- **Autocorrect targets**: a blacklisted word is never chosen as the word autocorrect replaces your typo with, even if it would otherwise be the best-scoring candidate. The same holds for the two halves of a missing-space split correction.

The blacklist never touches the word itself as text. Typing it, editing it, and sending it all work exactly as normal. Blacklisting only removes a word from being *offered*, which makes it a suggestion filter rather than a content filter.

:::note
"Block offensive words," a separate toggle under <SettingsPath path="Typing / Automatic corrections / Block offensive words" />, uses this exact same filtering mechanism. It runs against a bundled list of profanity and slurs instead of words you typed in yourself. It's on by default, but it only shows up in the Typing screen while autocorrect is turned on, even though (like the blacklist) it also affects plain suggestions. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/) for the rest of the automatic-corrections settings.
:::

## Details & edge cases

- **Nothing is blacklisted by default.** The list starts empty and you build it entry by entry.
- **It's mostly independent of your personal dictionary, with one automatic link.** The blacklist and the [personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) of learned words are two separate stores, and neither settings screen offers a shortcut to push a word from one into the other. But blacklisting a word does purge it from the personal dictionary and from the words still waiting to be learned, the moment the keyboard picks up the change, and it stays out of both while it remains blacklisted. There's also a link on the keyboard itself: **Delete** on a held suggestion forgets the word from the personal dictionary and, when the word is still in a downloaded or imported list, puts it on the blacklist as well, since that is the only way to unlist one word from a read-only list. As a *candidate*, meaning something offered to complete another word or something autocorrect would replace a typo with, the blacklist always wins, so the word stays suppressed either way. But if you type that exact word yourself, autocorrect leaves it alone regardless of the blacklist, the same as it would for any word already known to the keyboard.
- **Removing a word un-blocks it immediately.** Blacklisting it in the first place already purged it from your personal dictionary, so taking it off the blacklist starts the word over rather than restoring what it had learned before. You don't need to touch the personal dictionary at all.
- **A word doesn't need history to be blacklisted.** You can blacklist any word, learned or not. The blacklist doesn't check whether the word exists in any dictionary first.
- **Backed up like any other setting.** The list lives in the same settings store as everything else in the app, so it's included automatically in a [settings backup](/privacy/backup/). There's no separate export step for it.
- **An email field is the one gap.** In a field the app marked as an email address, the strip is driven straight from your contacts' addresses rather than through the ordinary suggestion pass, and that shortcut doesn't consult the blacklist. Blacklisting an address keeps it out of the strip everywhere else, but not there. See [Contact-aware typing](/smart/contacts/) for what that path does.

For the rest of the suggestion strip (completions, corrections, and next-word prediction), see [Suggestions & prediction](/smart/suggestions/). For teaching the keyboard new words instead of blocking them, see [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/).
